Primavista: Why the name?

There's a moment in music that many players quietly dread: someone puts a sheet of music in front of you that you've never seen before, and you play it right away. No rehearsal, no preparation, just as it stands on the page. In Italian this is called a prima vista, "at first sight." In English we call it sight-reading.

That same promise sits at the heart of every WYSIWYG editor. What you see on the sheet is what you hear, or in our world, what ends up on the page. No mental translation, no "it'll look different later." The content is the score, the published page is the performance, and there should be no surprises between the two.

The name is also a small wink at the ecosystem. In the PHP world you compose with Composer and build with Symfony, and sooner or later every orchestra needs someone to make the music readable. Primavista aims to be that music stand: an editor that feels just as much at home in Symfony UX as it does in React, and that lets editors play their content right the first time.

So the goal is simple: open the page and start playing. A prima vista.

Tagline ideas:

"What you see is what you play."
"Content, straight from the sheet."
"Sight-read your content."
